THE GAME THAT STARTED IT ALL.

In 2005, a team of students at DigiPen made a strange little puzzle game about a princess, a living dungeon, and two connected doorways. The idea would become Portal.

CASE FILE / THE ORIGIN

THE FIRST PORTALS.

An experiment in movement and perspective that existed before Chell, GLaDOS, the handheld portal device, and Aperture Science.

01 / A PRINCESS WHO CAN'T JUMP

Princess No-Knees is trapped inside Wally, a sentient dungeon. To escape she must use the interconnected magical openings that appear in its walls, ceilings, and floors. Where a typical platformer would ask you to jump, Narbacular Drop asks you to change where a doorway leads.

02 / TWO PORTALS, ONE SPACE

The two portals let the player cross the room, redirect movement, and rethink the geometry of each challenge. The game was built around the mechanic rather than an elaborate story, making the portals themselves the main attraction.

03 / FROM CLASS PROJECT TO PORTAL

Valve representatives discovered the game at DigiPen's career event and invited the team to demonstrate it at Valve. After seeing the presentation, Gabe Newell offered to hire the students. The team began work on Portal at Valve in July 2005, bringing the central idea into the Source engine.

From DigiPen's own history and Kim Swift's development retrospective.
